## When they say no

**Constructive denial.** If the statutory deadline passes with no determination, administrative remedies are deemed exhausted for federal FOIA and you may proceed. In practice: file the appeal anyway, in writing, citing the missed deadline.

**Appeal, always.** Appeals are free, take fifteen minutes, and agencies release material on appeal at meaningful rates because a different office reviews it. Never accept a first denial as final.

**Glomar** ("can neither confirm nor deny"). Rare outside national security. If it appears on a *procurement* request, that is itself newsworthy — say so publicly.

**Overbreadth rejection.** Answer with a narrowing offer within 48 hours. An agency that rejects a narrowed request twice has a weaker position on appeal.

**Fee estimate shock.** A five-figure estimate is a soft denial. Respond by narrowing date ranges and custodians, and renew the fee-waiver argument citing your CC0 publication practice.

## FILE 5 — The price of the corpus, and the moat it built

**Docket:** Provenance and standing | **Class:** P1

This file is the one that most directly tests your Necropolis thesis — and it partly cuts against you. Record it anyway.

**Bartz v. Anthropic** (N.D. Cal., No. 3:24-cv-05417).
